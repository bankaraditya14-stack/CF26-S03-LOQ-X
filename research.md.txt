# Research — Urban Infrastructure Cascade Simulator (S-03)

## Document Purpose

This document defines the problem, engineering interpretation, research findings,
assumptions, simulation concepts, and recommended scope for building an
Urban Infrastructure Cascade Simulator for the hackathon.

The goal is to understand the problem correctly before creating the PRD,
TRD, architecture, and implementation.

---

# 1. Problem Statement

## Official Requirement

Develop an Urban Infrastructure Cascade Simulator that represents
interdependent urban services as a dynamic graph and simulates how
disruptions propagate through the system.

The simulator must:

- Represent urban services and dependencies as a dynamic graph.
- Support time-dependent system state.
- Simulate failures and recovery actions.
- Support multiple simultaneous disruptions.
- Measure cascade depth.
- Measure affected services.
- Measure recovery time.
- Provide reproducible simulation scenarios.

---

# 2. What Problem Are We Actually Solving?

[FACT]

Urban services are interconnected.

A failure in one service can affect other services that depend on it.

For example:

Electricity Failure
        ↓
Water Pump Failure
        ↓
Water Supply Degradation
        ↓
Hospital Service Impact

The important problem is not simply detecting:

> "Electricity is down."

The important problem is understanding:

> "What other services will be affected because electricity is down,
> through which dependency paths, after how much time, and how can
> recovery actions limit the cascade?"

[INTERP]

Therefore, this project is a simulation and decision-support system,
not simply a monitoring dashboard.

---

# 3. Why Traditional Monitoring Is Not Enough

[INTERP]

Traditional monitoring systems often monitor individual services separately.

For example:

Power monitoring:
    Power = FAILED

Water monitoring:
    Water = HEALTHY

Telecom monitoring:
    Telecom = HEALTHY

Each system can know its own status.

However, without a dependency model, the system may not understand:

Power Failure
    ↓
Water Pump
    ↓
Water Distribution
    ↓
Hospital

The simulator provides the missing cross-service dependency view.

It answers:

- What will fail next?
- Why will it fail?
- When will it fail?
- How deep will the cascade go?
- Which services are most affected?
- Which recovery action can stop the cascade?

---

# 4. What Is an Urban Service?

[INTERP]

An urban service is a system or infrastructure component that provides
an important function to citizens or city operations.

Possible services include:

- Electricity
- Water Pumping
- Water Distribution
- Telecom / Network
- Traffic Signals
- Emergency Dispatch
- Hospitals
- Sewage / Wastewater
- Public Transport
- Fuel Stations
- Municipal Control Systems

These are simulation entities.

They do not represent real operational infrastructure.

---

# 5. What Does "Interdependent" Mean?

[INTERP]

Two services are interdependent when one service requires another
service to operate.

Example:

Water Pump
    requires
Electricity

Therefore:

Electricity → Water Pump

Another example:

Emergency Dispatch
    requires
Telecom + Traffic Signals

Therefore:

Telecom → Emergency Dispatch

Traffic Signals → Emergency Dispatch

A service may depend on multiple other services.

---

# 6. Dynamic Graph

[FACT]

The simulator must represent urban services and their dependencies
as a dynamic graph.

[INTERP]

The graph contains:

## Nodes

Nodes represent services/infrastructure.

Example:

POWER_SUBSTATION
WATER_PUMP
WATER_NETWORK
TELECOM
HOSPITAL
TRAFFIC_SIGNAL

## Edges

Edges represent dependencies.

Example:

POWER_SUBSTATION
        ↓
WATER_PUMP
        ↓
WATER_NETWORK
        ↓
HOSPITAL

The graph is dynamic because node states change during simulation.

---

# 7. Node State Model

[REC]

Use a simple state model:

HEALTHY
DEGRADED
FAILED
RECOVERING

Optional:

RESTORED

Example:

T+0:
Power = HEALTHY
Water Pump = HEALTHY
Hospital = HEALTHY

T+5:
Power = FAILED
Water Pump = DEGRADED

T+10:
Water Pump = FAILED
Water Supply = DEGRADED

T+15:
Hospital = DEGRADED

This makes the time-based cascade visible.

---

# 8. Failure Propagation

[INTERP]

A failure should not automatically make every dependent node fail
at the same moment.

Instead, the simulator should apply:

- Dependency relationship
- Propagation delay
- Failure threshold
- Node state
- Recovery state

Example:

Power fails at T+0.

Water Pump requires Power.

Propagation delay = 5 simulation minutes.

Therefore:

T+0:
Power → FAILED

T+5:
Water Pump → FAILED

T+10:
Water Distribution → DEGRADED

T+15:
Hospital → DEGRADED

This creates a realistic-looking cascade timeline.

IMPORTANT:

The timing values are simulation parameters.

They are NOT claims about real infrastructure.

---

# 9. Multiple Simultaneous Disruptions

[FACT]

The simulator must support multiple disruptions at the same time.

Example:

T+0:

Electricity = FAILED
Telecom = FAILED

Both failures can propagate through the graph.

Example:

Electricity
   ↓
Water Pump
   ↓
Water Supply

Telecom
   ↓
Emergency Dispatch

Both cascades may eventually affect:

Hospital
Emergency Services
Public Transport

The simulator must merge these effects instead of treating them
as completely independent simulations.

---

# 10. Shared Dependency Example

A node can depend on multiple services.

Example:

Emergency Dispatch requires:

Telecom
+
Power

Therefore:

Telecom ─────┐
             ↓
        Emergency
        Dispatch
             ↑
Power ───────┘

If either dependency fails:

Emergency Dispatch may become DEGRADED.

If both dependencies fail:

Emergency Dispatch may become FAILED.

This gives the simulator more interesting dependency logic than
a simple linear chain.

---

# 11. Cascade Depth

[FACT]

The simulator must measure cascade depth.

[INTERP]

Cascade depth means the longest downstream dependency chain
created by an initial disruption.

Example:

Power
 ↓
Water Pump
 ↓
Water Supply
 ↓
Hospital

Cascade depth = 3 downstream transitions.

The exact counting convention should be documented in the PRD.

Recommended:

Depth 0 = initial disruption
Depth 1 = directly affected service
Depth 2 = next dependent service
Depth 3 = next dependent service

---

# 12. Affected Services

[FACT]

The simulator must measure affected services.

[INTERP]

Affected services are services that move from HEALTHY into
DEGRADED, FAILED, or another non-healthy state because of
the simulated disruption.

Example:

Initial failure:

Power

Affected:

Water Pump
Water Supply
Hospital
Traffic Signals

Affected Services = 4

Recommended UI:

Affected Services
      4

and show the actual affected nodes.

---

# 13. Recovery Time

[FACT]

The simulator must measure recovery time.

[INTERP]

Recovery time is the simulated duration required for a failed
service or the overall system to return to a defined healthy state.

Example:

Power fails at:

T+0

Recovery starts:

T+20

Power restored:

T+30

Recovery time:

10 simulation minutes

The UI should show:

Recovery Time
00:10

The system should also be able to show:

- Time to first failure
- Time to peak impact
- Time to recovery

---

# 14. Recovery Actions

[FACT]

The simulator must support recovery actions.

[INTERP]

Recovery actions are interventions that attempt to restore or
protect infrastructure.

Examples:

- Restore power
- Activate backup generator
- Restore telecom
- Reroute water supply
- Activate emergency control
- Isolate failed node
- Restore a damaged infrastructure component

Example:

Without recovery:

Power
 ↓
Water Pump
 ↓
Water Supply
 ↓
Hospital
 ↓
Emergency Services

With recovery:

Power fails
 ↓
Backup generator activated
 ↓
Water Pump remains operational
 ↓
Cascade stopped

This is one of the most important parts of the demo.

---

# 15. Simulation Scenarios

[FACT]

The simulator must provide reproducible simulation scenarios.

[INTERP]

A scenario should contain:

- Scenario name
- Graph configuration
- Initial failures
- Simulation start time
- Propagation rules
- Recovery actions
- Random seed, if randomness is ever introduced

Example:

Scenario:
"Monsoon Power Failure"

Initial failures:
POWER_SUBSTATION_01

Duration:
60 simulation minutes

Recovery:
Backup Generator at T+15

Running the same scenario with the same configuration
must produce the same result.

---

# 16. Deterministic Simulation

[REC]

The first version should be deterministic.

Avoid unnecessary randomness.

Same:

Graph
+
Initial failures
+
Parameters
+
Recovery actions

should produce:

Same timeline
Same cascade depth
Same affected services
Same recovery time

This makes the demo reproducible and easier to debug.

[ASSUMPTION]

The project should be designed for an Indian urban context.

However:

# 21. Simulation Engine

[REC]

The core engine can use discrete simulation ticks.

Example:

simulationTime = 0

Every tick:

1. Find failed/degraded nodes.
2. Check dependent nodes.
3. Apply dependency rules.
4. Apply propagation delays.
5. Update node states.
6. Apply scheduled recovery actions.
7. Record state changes.
8. Calculate metrics.
9. Advance simulation time.

Example:

T+0
 ↓
Evaluate dependencies
 ↓
Schedule next failures
 ↓
T+5
 ↓
Apply failures
 ↓
Evaluate dependencies
 ↓
T+10
 ↓
Continue

This is sufficient for a hackathon prototype.

---

# 22. Dependency Rules

[REC]

Each dependency edge should contain information such as:

{
  source: "POWER",
  target: "WATER_PUMP",
  dependencyType: "REQUIRES",
  propagationDelay: 5,
  failureThreshold: 1
}

Possible dependency types:

REQUIRES
SUPPORTS
COMMUNICATES_WITH
CONTROLS
BACKUP_FOR

For the first version, only REQUIRES is necessary.

Additional types can be added if time permits.

---

# 23. Failure Rule

[REC]

Simple initial rule:

If a required dependency fails,
the dependent service becomes DEGRADED.

If all critical dependencies fail,
the dependent service becomes FAILED.

Example:

Hospital requires:

Power
Water

Case 1:

Power = FAILED
Water = HEALTHY

Hospital = DEGRADED

Case 2:

Power = FAILED
Water = FAILED

Hospital = FAILED

This gives the simulator meaningful behavior without requiring
complex mathematical infrastructure models.

---

# 24. Recovery Rule

[REC]

When a dependency is restored:

The dependent service should not necessarily become HEALTHY
immediately.

Possible transition:

FAILED
 ↓
RECOVERING
 ↓
HEALTHY

This allows recovery time to be simulated.

Example:

Power restored at T+30.

Water Pump:

T+30 → RECOVERING

T+35 → HEALTHY

# 28. What Makes This Different From a Dashboard?

A normal dashboard says:

Power: DOWN
Water: DOWN
Hospital: DEGRADED

Our simulator says:

Power failed at T+0.

Because Water Pump depends on Power,
Water Pump failed at T+5.

Because Water Supply depends on Water Pump,
Water Supply degraded at T+10.

Because Hospital depends on Water,
Hospital degraded at T+15.

Therefore:

Cascade Depth = 3
Affected Services = 3
Peak Impact = 3

This causal explanation is the core value of the project.

# 38. Important Design Principle

The simulation engine must be separated from the UI.

The UI should not contain the actual cascade logic.

Instead:

Simulation Engine
       ↓
Simulation State
       ↓
UI

This allows:

- Easier testing
- Easier debugging
- Reproducible results
- Scenario comparison
- Future backend integration

## Reproducibility Principle

The simulator must be deterministic.

Given the same:
- graph definition
- initial failures
- propagation parameters
- recovery actions
- simulation configuration

the simulator must produce the same event sequence, state transitions, and metrics.

The core simulation must not depend on:
- real-world clock
- network responses
- unseeded randomness
- browser timing
- unpredictable processing order


## Failure and Recovery Model

A service can transition through:

HEALTHY → DEGRADED → FAILED → RECOVERING → HEALTHY

Not every scenario must use every state.

Recovery is simulated rather than instantaneous.
Recovery actions have configurable simulated durations.


## Data Integrity

All infrastructure nodes, dependencies, timings, service states,
and scenarios are synthetic simulation data.

The project does not claim to represent the actual dependency
structure of any Indian city or government infrastructure.

Simulation timings are illustrative parameters, not real-world
measurements.

There must be only one source of simulation truth:

SimulationState.

No UI component may independently calculate cascade,
